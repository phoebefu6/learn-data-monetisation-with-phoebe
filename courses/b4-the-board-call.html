Part 1 · covers reading the ladder

Five rungs, read as a board would

5 min live

A single forecast invites a single argument: is the number right? A ladder invites the argument a board should have: which change matters most? Each rung starts from the base case and changes one thing, so the gap between a rung and the base is the weight of that one thing. A board does not need to check the arithmetic. It needs to find the rung the decision turns on, and then ask who controls it.

LiveWhat each rung tells a board3 min▶

1 · Base case. +40.3k of cash over five years, break-even at 4.8 years, and 56.4 percent of the 5,000 draws never break even within five years. A thin yes on the point number and a coin toss on the range.

2 · The sponsor's case. Price 40, seven new customers a year, 8 percent lost: +1362.4k. It is the version an enthusiastic sponsor brings, packaged so the board can see how much of the case rests on optimism nobody has tested.

3 · Consent and anonymity tightened. One governance change: the privacy notice gap rises to 20 percent of records and the smallest publishable cell rises to 25. Cells published fall from 92.6 to 68.7 percent, and the five years end at -386.7k, with 98.2 percent of draws never breaking even. Nobody in sales can move this rung back.

4 · One exclusive licensee. +420.0k, break-even at 2.2 years, 0.0 percent of draws without break-even. The guarantee is a floor; Part 3 shows what it costs in a strong market.

5 · Every input held certain. The break button. The same +40.3k as the base, with every draw identical, so the range collapses to one point. It shows what a single-number P&L quietly assumes.

LiveWhich rung the decision turns on2 min▶

Ask of each rung: if this happened, would we decide differently, and who controls whether it happens? The sponsor's case would make the decision easy, but it is a hope, not a risk. The exclusive licensee is an option Keelson can choose. The tightened rung is neither: it is a state of the world Keelson can be moved to by a regulator, a privacy review or a complaint, and it turns a thin yes into a clear loss. That makes the consent rung the one the decision turns on.

The mechanism is worth saying out loud in the board room. The price of an index scales with how much of the grid it covers: at 68.7 percent of cells against 92.6 percent, the index is worth 74.1 percent of the base, so a 32.0k price behaves like 23.7k. Consent is not a compliance footnote to this P&L; it is an input to the price.

Self-studyWhy the range matters more than the point2 min▶

The base case and the break rung print the same five-year cash, +40.3k. A board shown only that number would treat them as the same proposal. They are not: the base case has a P10 of -364.0k and a P90 of +347.6k, and the break rung has neither. The way the desk draws the range, and how Business Cases reads a range against the option to wait, is in Business Cases, Analyst session 5 ↗. The board-level habit is simpler: never accept a forecast without its no-break-even share.

Real world

The rung nobody asked for. Sefa Adjei's first draft of the board pack showed the base case and the sponsor's case. Then Keelson's privacy team questioned whether the 2019 privacy notice covered selling an index built from claims at all. Maya asked the Analyst team to add a rung for a tightened notice and a stricter cell threshold before the pack went out. That rung became the first slide. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Part 2 · covers the risk register a data deal needs

Every risk gets an owner and a trigger

5 min live

A ladder says what happens if a risk lands. A register says who is watching for it and what they are watching. Each row needs three things: a name a board member would recognise, one owner by role, and a trigger, the observable event that means the risk has started to happen. Likelihood scores are the part most registers invent; leave them out unless you have a basis, and place risks against the ladder instead.

LiveSix rows a data deal needs3 min▶

Consent drift. The notice the data was collected under changes, or complaints about reuse rise, and the sellable share falls. Owner: the chief data officer. Trigger: a notice revision or a complaints count crossing an agreed line. If it fires, Keelson is on rung 3. The operational side of notices and consent is taught in PDPA & DNC ↗.

Re-identification. A small cell slips through, or a buyer joins the index to data it already holds. Owner: the data protection officer. The cell-size threshold is one control; it does not make the data anonymous on its own, so the register also needs contract terms on what buyers may combine. The maths of anonymisation belongs to Privacy Engineering (planned).

Regulatory. Under GDPR Art 83(5), infringements of the lawful-basis and consent articles can draw fines of up to 20m EUR or 4 percent of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Singapore's PDPA has its own regime; see PDPA & DNC ↗. Owner: general counsel.

Competitive. At the best single price, 32.0k, the biggest buyer group is smaller insurers: 8 of the 18 buyers. Selling them a regional repair cost index helps them price against Keelson. Owner: the COO.

Concentration. One licensee, or two, becomes most of the revenue line, and the renewal date becomes a cliff. Owner: the CFO.

Partner dependency. A partner runs refresh, support or the customer contract, and Keelson cannot easily take it back. Owner: the head of partnerships.

Self-studyWhy no likelihood numbers2 min▶

A register that scores consent drift "likely" or "3 out of 5" looks rigorous and is usually a guess with a decimal point. Unless the organisation has a history of the event to count, a likelihood score invites the board to argue about the number instead of the trigger. The ladder already gives the size of each risk in cash; the register adds who watches for it and what they watch. Where a risk has no rung yet, re-identification for instance, say so and ask for one.

Two risks on the register are also choices. Concentration is what you accept when you take the exclusive licensee; competitive exposure is what you accept when you sell to smaller insurers. Write the choice down next to the risk, so the board sees that someone decided it.

Real world

Concentration, in a filing. Reddit's S-1 (February 2024) reported data licensing contracts entered in January 2024 with an aggregate contract value of $203.0 million over two to three years, naming no counterparty; a Google deal was reportedly worth about $60 million a year. Its 10-K for 2025 says substantially all of that licensing value comes from two partners, and its 10-Q filed 30 October 2026 says those agreements are "approaching the end of their respective current terms". Two partners, one renewal window: that is the concentration row, written by the company itself.

Part 3 · covers the go or no-go paper and the hand-off

One page, one decision, one hand-off

5 min live

The base case breaks even on cash at 4.8 years. At Keelson's 9 percent hurdle its NPV is -77.9k. Both are true; they answer different questions. Break-even asks when the money comes back; NPV asks whether it comes back fast enough to beat the next-best use of the capital. Judging that is the job of a business case, so the paper hands the cash flows over rather than re-arguing them.

LiveFour options, written side by side3 min▶

Go direct. The base case: +40.3k over five years, NPV -77.9k, and a 56.4 percent chance of no break-even within five years. Keelson keeps the upside and carries every risk on the register.

Go through one exclusive licensee. A floor: +420.0k and no draw without break-even. But the floor is also a cap. With the sponsor's inputs, selling direct ends year 5 at +1362.4k and the exclusive licence at +953.4k. The guarantee wins in a thin market and caps a strong one, and it puts the concentration row on the register.

Pilot one segment first. Sell to one buyer group, say repair networks or parts makers, which do not compete with Keelson, before committing the full build. The cost of a pilot is time; the value of waiting is a question Business Cases answers properly.

No-go, and keep the data on the improve road. The motor claims history still prices Keelson's own policies. A no-go is a result, not a failure, and the improve road is where most data returns come from. Data Strategy ↗ owns that road.

LiveBreak-even, NPV and the hand-off2 min▶

The desk prints the base case's cash flows: -330.0k, -44.3k, +28.2k, +83.8k, +131.1k, +171.4k. Added up, cumulative cash turns positive in year 5. Discounted at 9 percent, the same flows sum to -77.9k, because the money arrives late and late money is worth less. So the board sentence is not "it pays back", it is "it pays back in cash but not at our hurdle on the base case".

What a funder does with that, whether a different rate applies, how to read IRR against payback, is taught in Business Cases, Analyst session 3 ↗, and the value of piloting first in Analyst session 5 ↗. The paper's job is to make the cash flows honest and the decision clear; the business case's job is to judge them.

Self-studyEvidence that would change the decision2 min▶

A good paper names, in advance, what would make the board decide differently. For Keelson, two pieces of evidence would move the answer. A refreshed privacy notice, reviewed by the privacy team, that clearly covers aggregated reuse would take rung 3 off the table, or at least narrow it. A signed design partner, a repair network or parts maker willing to pay for the first year, would turn the base case's customer count from an assumption into an observation.

Writing the evidence down first protects the board from moving the goalposts later, and protects the sponsor from an open-ended "come back when you are more certain".

Real world

Ines's question. At the board, Ines Carvalho did not ask whether +40.3k was right. She asked why a project that pays back in five years had a negative NPV at Keelson's own hurdle, and whether a pilot with one design partner would cost less than finding out at full scale. Maya's paper already had the answer in its last block, and the board approved a pilot with repair networks, subject to the refreshed notice. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Homework

After the Leader track

30 min
  1. Find your consent rung. For your candidate dataset, name the one governance change that would most reduce what you could sell, and who in your organisation could trigger it.
  2. Fill in the owners. For each of your three register rows, find the real person in that role and ask whether they would accept the trigger you wrote.
  3. Find your hurdle rate. Ask finance what rate your organisation discounts new investments at, and whether a data product would be treated differently.
  4. Write the evidence line. Name one document or signature that, if it existed, would change your decision, and how long it would take to get.

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The break rung, every input held certain, prints +40.3k, the same as the base case. What does it show a board?

Same cash, no range. The break button shows what every single-number P&L quietly assumes.

2 · The base case breaks even on cash at 4.8 years, yet its NPV at Keelson's 9 percent hurdle is -77.9k. Why can both be true?

They answer different questions. Judging which one governs is the business case's job, taught in Business Cases, Analyst session 3.

3 · Why does a consent and anonymity rung belong on every data deal ladder?

It is the rung a privacy review or a complaint can move you to. A threshold is one control, never anonymity on its own, and no rung comes with a likelihood.
