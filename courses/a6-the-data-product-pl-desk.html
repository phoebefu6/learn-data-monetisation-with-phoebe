Part 0

Where we start

The Keelson Repair Cost Index is a quarterly index of motor repair cost and claim frequency by region and vehicle class, built from Keelson Mutual's own claims and sold to fleet operators, repair networks, parts makers and smaller insurers. Every earlier widget fed one input into tonight's desk: the share of cells that survive consent and the k threshold (Analyst session 2) scales the price, the platform's fixed cost (session 3) is the run line, the 32.0k value price (session 4) is the base price, and the partner commission (session 5) comes off revenue. Every Keelson input is invented, in thousands of currency units per year.

Part 1 · covers the data product P&L

How the desk adds up one year

5 min live

A data product P&L has the shape of a subscription business: a build cost up front, a fixed cost to keep the pipeline, the steward and the legal review running, a customer base that grows by new wins and shrinks by losses, and a small cost to serve each customer. What makes it a data product is the line most models leave out: the price depends on how much of the data you are allowed to publish.

LiveThe five lines, and where each came from3 min▶

Customers. Six in year 1, then four new each year, with 15 percent of the base lost each year. That path reaches 15.9 customers by year 5, still under the 18 buyers who would pay 32.0k in Analyst session 4: the desk never assumes more customers than the price search found.

Price. 32.0k a year, the best single API price from session 4, multiplied by the share of cells you are allowed to publish over the base 92.6 percent. Tighten consent and the price falls with the product.

Fixed run cost. 150.0k a year: the shared platform from session 3 (pipeline, data steward, DPIA, legal review). It does not care how many customers you have.

Cost to serve and cost to win. 8.0k per customer per year, and 5.0k for each new customer Keelson signs itself. Customers who come through a partner cost Keelson nothing to win, which is part of what the commission pays for.

Build. 330.0k in year 0: the platform (120.0k), the report tier (40.0k), the API tier (160.0k) and 10.0k of legal set-up.

Self-studyWhy the margin looks wonderful and the cash does not2 min▶

Revenue minus cost to serve is 72.5 percent of revenue in every year: a copy of a dataset costs almost nothing to deliver. That figure is the one that gets quoted in strategy decks. It hides the fixed run cost, which at six customers is larger than the revenue. A data product's economics live in the fixed line and the customer count, not in the gross margin. Analyst session 3 drew the same thing as the cost curve: 59.2k per customer at five customers, 14.3k at forty.

Real world

The margin slide. Sefa Adjei, Keelson's head of partnerships, opened the first index pitch with "a data product with a 72 percent gross margin". Ines Carvalho, the CFO, asked one question: "How many customers before the fixed cost is covered?" The desk answers it: the cumulative cash does not turn positive until late in year 5. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Part 2 · covers the five rungs

Five rungs, one change each

5 min live

A single P&L invites a single argument: is it right? A ladder invites a better one: which change matters? Each rung starts from the base case and changes one thing, so the difference between any rung and the base is the price of that one thing. The last rung is a break button: it holds every input certain, and the range collapses to a point, which is what every single-number P&L quietly assumes.

LiveWhat each rung is for3 min▶

1 · Base case. The inputs from sessions 2 to 5. Ends year 5 at +40.3k cumulative cash, break-even at 4.8 years.

2 · The sponsor's case. The version a keen sponsor brings: price 40.0k, seven new customers a year, 8 percent lost. +1362.4k. Its job is to show how much of the case rests on optimism you have not tested.

3 · Consent and anonymity tightened. One governance change: the privacy notice gap rises to 20 percent of records and the smallest publishable cell rises to 25. Cells published fall from 92.6 to 68.7 percent, the price falls with them, and the five years end at -386.7k. This is the rung a regulator, a privacy review or a single complaint can move you to.

4 · One exclusive licensee. Sell everything through one partner for the larger of a 300.0k guarantee and 20 percent of their revenue. +420.0k, break-even at 2.2 years, and no draw that fails to break even.

5 · Every input held certain. The break button: same inputs as the base, no uncertainty. It prints +40.3k with a P10 and a P90 of +40.3k as well, which is a lie the base case's 56.4 percent chance of no break-even exposes.

Self-studyWhy one change per rung2 min▶

When a rung changes three things at once, nobody can say which one moved the answer, and the meeting argues about all three. The sponsor's case is the deliberate exception, labelled as such: it packages the optimistic inputs a sponsor tends to bring together, so the board can see their combined weight. If a reviewer asks which of the three matters most, move them one at a time on the desk; Business Cases does the same thing systematically with a tornado, Analyst session 4 ↗.

Real world

The rung nobody had built. Keelson's first index model had a base case and a sponsor's case. Maya Okafor, the CDO, asked for the consent rung after the privacy team said the 2019 motor policy notice might not cover analytics reuse. It was the only rung that turned the five-year cash negative, and it moved the conversation from price to the privacy notice. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Part 3 · covers the range and the hand-off

Breaks even, and still fails the hurdle

5 min live

The base case turns cash-positive in year 5. It also has a net present value of -77.9k at Keelson's 9 percent hurdle. Both are true, and they answer different questions: break-even asks when the money comes back; NPV asks whether it comes back fast enough to beat what Keelson could earn elsewhere. The desk prints both and leaves the second argument to the people whose job it is.

LiveReading the range on the desk3 min▶

Each of the ten inputs is drawn from a triangular range around the value you set (the ranges are in the source map), 5,000 times, with the same seed every time so a colleague gets the same numbers. The desk prints the P10, P50 and P90 of the five-year cumulative cash and the share of draws that never break even.

For the base case: P10 -364.0k, P50 -47.3k, P90 +347.6k, and 56.4 percent of draws do not break even within five years. The middle draw is below the base case's +40.3k because several ranges reach further on the bad side than the good: the build can overrun to 430.0k but cannot fall below 300.0k, and losses can reach 25 percent a year.

Self-studyThe hand-off, and what not to re-teach2 min▶

The desk prints a line under the range: "Cash flows to take to a business case", six numbers from year 0 to year 5. For the base case they are -330.0k, -44.3k, +28.2k, +83.8k, +131.1k, +171.4k. Discounted at 9 percent they sum to -77.9k. What a funder does with that, whether a different rate applies, how to read IRR against payback, how to put a value on waiting for a pilot, is taught in Business Cases, Analyst session 3 ↗ and session 5 ↗. The desk's job is to make the cash flows honest; the business case's job is to judge them.

Real world

Two true sentences. Sefa told the steering group the index "pays back in under five years". Ines added that at Keelson's 9 percent hurdle it destroys 77.9k of value in the base case. Both sentences came off the same desk. The decision paper carried both, with the consent rung beside them. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Homework

After the desk

30 min
  1. Build your own ladder. For a data product you know, write a base case and four rungs that each change one thing, one of them a governance change. Your numbers will differ.
  2. Name your break button. Find a P&L in your organisation that shows one number and no range. Write the one input most likely to move it.
  3. Take the cash flows to finance. Ask what hurdle rate your company uses for a new product line, and whether a data product gets the same one.
  4. Write the board sentence. One sentence: what the base case says, and which rung decides the answer.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Analyst session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The base case breaks even on cash at 4.8 years and has an NPV of -77.9k at 9 percent. What is the honest reading?

Break-even asks when; NPV asks whether it beats the alternative use of the money. The business case weighs them; the desk prints both.

2 · Rungs 1 and 5 both print +40.3k. What separates them?

The point estimate is identical. The break button shows what a single number hides: more than half the plausible futures never break even.

3 · Why does tightening consent and anonymity lower the price on the desk, not just the volume?

A data product's value is its coverage. Suppressed regions and vehicle classes are the detail buyers wanted.
