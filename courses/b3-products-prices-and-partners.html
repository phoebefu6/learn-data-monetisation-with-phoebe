Products, prices and partners

Once you know a dataset may be sold, three decisions remain, and they are usually made in the wrong order. What exactly is the product: rows, an index, a report, a feed? What is it worth to each buyer, and what does a price do to who buys? And who sells it: you, a reseller, or one licensee with a guarantee? Tonight: the packaging ladder, pricing on value, and the three routes to market with their floors and caps. Keelson Mutual is constructed.

Part 1 · covers the packaging ladder

From rows to answers: what exactly are we selling?

5 min live

"Sell the data" hides a choice between six different products. At the bottom of the ladder the buyer gets raw records and does all the work. At the top, the answer arrives inside the buyer's own software and the records never leave. Each rung up carries more of the buyer's decision and less of the raw record, which is also less privacy exposure. CISR said it in one line: selling "is not just about selling data; it's also about selling insights".

LiveSix rungs, and what each one hands the buyer3 min▶

Raw records. The buyer gets rows and does the analysis. It is the rung Leader session 2 makes hardest: every record has to be lawful to pass on, and the buyer can do anything with it. Doug Laney's direct monetisation list includes it plainly, "selling raw data through brokers or data markets". For personal data, it is rarely allowed.

Aggregated data. Counts and averages by group, with small cells suppressed. The buyer still builds the answer, but no single person is in the product.

An index. One number per region, class and quarter that tracks something the buyer cares about. Keelson's Repair Cost Index sits here: motor repair cost and claim frequency by region and vehicle class, quarterly.

Insight and reports. Analysis written for the buyer's question. Laney's list again: "offering insights, analyses and reports".

An API feed. The same content, delivered by query into the buyer's systems on their schedule.

Embedded. The answer appears inside the buyer's own product or workflow. The buyer may never see a row.

Climbing the ladder costs you more to build and serve, and earns you more of the buyer's decision. It also moves the product away from the record, which is where most of the legal risk lives. For a wider tour of data product types, see Data Products ↗; here the ladder is only a selling decision.

Self-studyKeelson's four tiers, and why their costs differ2 min▶

Keelson plans to sell the index in four forms: a quarterly report, a dashboard, an API feed and bespoke analysis. Each sits on a different rung and costs a different amount to build and to serve one more customer. Analyst session 3 costs them on a shared platform: with the planned mix, serving one more customer costs 7.9k a year, and the full cost per customer falls from 59.2k at five customers to 14.3k at forty, because the platform's fixed cost is shared across more buyers. That curve is why a data product's economics live in the customer count, not in the cost of one copy.

CDAIO module 5 treats delivery format and pricing model as one decision, and the pairing matters: a feed queried on demand suits a different price shape than a report that arrives once a quarter.

Real world

A product built by pooling, not selling rows. Verisk describes ClaimSearch as a contributory "give to get" claims database: insurers contribute their claims and in return can search everyone's. By Verisk's own statement (blog, 31 July 2025) it holds over 1.8 billion US claims and covers about 95 percent of the US property and casualty market. Those are the vendor's numbers, not an audit. The lesson for packaging is the rung: members do not buy each other's raw files; they buy answers to a query, under a contract that binds every contributor.

Part 2 · covers pricing by value

Price on what the buyer gets, then look at who buys

5 min live

A copy of a dataset costs almost nothing to deliver, so its cost says little about its price. Jian Pei's survey of data pricing (2022) puts it in economic terms: information goods have "zero marginal costs" and are non-rival, one buyer's copy does not use up another's. So the price is set from the buyer's side. A working rule: willingness to pay is the value a buyer gets from the product in a year times a capture share, the part of that value a buyer will hand over. Keelson's capture share is 25 percent, a constructed assumption you can move on the widget.

LiveThe best single price, and the buyers it picks3 min▶

One price. Analyst session 4 searches every API price for the one that earns the most contribution, price minus the cost to serve. With 110 constructed prospects and a 25 percent capture share, the answer is 32.0k a year: 18 buyers, revenue 576.0k, contribution 414.0k. Check it: 18 x 32.0k = 576.0k, and 18 x (32.0k - 9.0k serve) = 414.0k.

The catch. Look at who the 18 are: fleet operators 0 of 60, repair networks 4 of 25, parts makers 6 of 10, smaller insurers 8 of 15. The fleet operators who started this conversation buy nothing at that price, and the largest group of buyers is smaller insurers, Keelson's competitors. That is question 2 from Leader session 1, would selling undercut our own use, arriving by way of the price.

Two tiers. Offer a Standard tier (the quarterly report, priced on half the value, cheap to serve) and a Pro tier (the API, priced on full value). The best pair is Standard 9.0k and Pro 25.0k: 18 Standard and 18 Pro, 36 buyers, revenue 612.0k, contribution 423.0k. Tiers double the buyers and add 9.0k of contribution. By segment, the Standard tier is what brings fleet operators in at all: 8 of them buy Standard, none buy Pro.

Self-studyWhy versions work for information goods2 min▶

Pei's survey lists the pricing models used for data: versioning, bundling, subscription, auctions and query-based pricing. Versioning works because a second version of an information good costs almost nothing to make, so you can offer a lighter version to buyers who value less and keep the full one for those who value more. Each buyer sorts themselves by choosing. Two tiers are versioning in its simplest form.

One point is ours, not Pei's: a data product has a high fixed cost even when one copy is free. Analyst session 3 drew it as the cost curve. Pricing on value tells you what buyers will pay; the cost curve tells you how many of them you need.

Consumer price elasticity and price tests on retail goods belong to a planned Pricing Analytics course. Here the buyer is a business, and the price comes from the value it gets.

Self-studyCost-plus is a floor, not a method2 min▶

At 16 customers, Keelson's full cost is 23.9k per customer; with a 30 percent markup that is 31.1k. It lands next to the 32.0k value price by coincidence, not by method. Change the customer count and the cost-plus price moves; change the buyers' value and the value price moves. Use cost to check that a price covers the product. Use value to set it.

Real world

The veto in the room. When Sefa showed the two-tier result, Tom Reyes, the COO, said no Pro tier for smaller insurers: the API, at full detail, is the version that helps a competitor price motor policies against Keelson. Ines Carvalho asked what the veto costs. Nobody knew yet, and that is the right order: name the buyer you will not serve, then price what you gave up. Working out that cost is step 2 of tonight's build-along. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Part 3 · covers who sells it

Direct, reseller or one licensee: who sells it?

5 min live

Keelson can sell the index itself, sell through a reseller who already reaches buyers, or license it to one partner exclusively for a guaranteed minimum. Analyst session 5 runs each route for one steady-state year across 5,000 seeded draws. In the middle outcome (P50): direct +304.8k, reseller +429.0k, exclusive +323.8k. The exclusive licence pays the guarantee in 41.0 percent of draws: it trades the upside for a floor.

LiveThree routes, one year each3 min▶

Sell direct. Keelson finds, signs and serves every customer. It keeps the whole price and pays every cost of winning and serving. P50 +304.8k, with a range from +240.2k (P10) to +366.9k (P90).

Through a reseller. A partner who already sells to fleets and repair networks brings more customers and takes a 30 percent commission; Keelson still serves them. P50 +429.0k, the highest middle outcome, and the widest range: +270.4k to +629.8k. The reseller also stands between Keelson and the buyer, so the contract has to say what the buyer may do with the data.

One exclusive licensee. One partner takes the index, sells it to its own customers, and pays Keelson the larger of a minimum guarantee and a share of its revenue. Nobody else may buy from Keelson. P50 +323.8k, and P10 +300.0k: the guarantee is a floor, and it binds in 41.0 percent of draws. The guarantee wins in a thin market and caps a strong one.

Analyst session 5 does the maths and the contract terms that carry it: field of use, term, audit rights, scope of exclusivity.

Self-studyWhat exclusivity costs when the market is strong2 min▶

One year hides the long cost of an exclusive licence. On the P&L desk in Analyst session 6, with the sponsor's optimistic inputs, selling direct ends year 5 at +1362.4k of cumulative cash; the exclusive licence ends at +953.4k. The same desk shows the other side: on the desk's ladder, 0.0% of the exclusive rung's draws fail to break even in five years, against 56.4% for the base case. That trade, a known floor against an unknown ceiling, is a board decision, and Leader session 4 puts it there.

Self-studyOne partner is a concentration risk, and the law does not set your price2 min▶

Concentration. A single licensee means a single renewal. If the partner walks away, or renegotiates at renewal knowing you have no one else, the revenue line goes with it. The Real world story below is the public version.

The EU Data Act is not a price rule for voluntary sales. Article 8 requires fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms, and Article 9 allows reasonable, non-discriminatory compensation that "may include a margin", but only where a data holder is obliged to make data available. A voluntary licence of an index is priced by negotiation. For operational detail on consent and data protection law, see Data Governance ↗.

Real world

Two partners and a renewal date. Reddit's S-1 (February 2024) disclosed data licensing contracts entered in January 2024 with an aggregate contract value of $203.0 million over two to three years, naming no counterparty. The Google deal was reportedly worth about $60 million a year (Reuters, citing unnamed sources). Its 10-K for fiscal 2025 says licensing revenue comes from two partners, and its 10-Q filed 30 October 2026 says those agreements are "approaching the end of their respective current terms". Large data licences can be real money, and two buyers is a concentration risk the filings themselves name.

Homework

Before Leader session 4

30 min
  1. Ask one buyer. Find one person in a likely buyer organisation and ask what decision your data would help them make, and roughly what that decision is worth to them in a year.
  2. Name your competitors in the list. For your candidate dataset, list every buyer segment and mark the ones that compete with you. Decide whether you would sell to them, and in which version.
  3. Find a reseller. Name one organisation that already sells to your buyers. Write what commission would make your direct route the better one.
  4. Read one licence. Find a data or content licence your organisation has signed, as buyer or seller, and copy out its term, field of use and exclusivity clause.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Why price a data product on the value the buyer gets rather than on what one copy costs to make?

Information goods have zero marginal cost (Pei). Cost tells you whether a price covers the product; value tells you what buyers will pay.

2 · Keelson's best single price, 32.0k, wins 18 buyers and the most contribution. What is wrong with it?

Smaller insurers are 8 of the 18; fleet operators are 0 of 60. A price picks your customers, so check who it picks against question 2 from Leader session 1.

3 · What does an exclusive licence with a minimum guarantee trade away?

The guarantee binds in 41.0 percent of draws, and with the sponsor's inputs the exclusive route ends five years at +953.4k against +1362.4k direct.
