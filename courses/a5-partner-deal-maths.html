Partner deal maths

A data product can reach its buyers three ways: you sell it yourself, a reseller sells it for a commission, or one partner licenses it exclusively for a guaranteed sum. Each route has its own formula, its own range and its own failure mode, and the contract decides which of those you live with. Tonight: the three formulas by hand, 5,000 seeded draws per route, and the six terms that carry the numbers into a signed deal. Keelson is constructed; the arithmetic runs in your browser.

Part 1 · covers three routes and their formulas

Three ways to reach a buyer, three formulas

5 min live

Every route starts from the same price, 32.0k a customer a year, and the same cost to serve, 8.0k. What changes is who finds the customer, who keeps how much of the price, and whether anyone else may buy. Write each route as one line of arithmetic before you simulate anything: if you cannot, you do not yet understand the deal.

LiveThe per-customer arithmetic, by hand3 min▶

Sell direct. Keelson keeps the whole price and pays both costs: 32.0k - 8.0k - 5.0k = 19.0k a customer. Multiply by the customers Keelson signs itself, somewhere between 10 and 22, most likely 16. At 16 customers: 16 x 19.0k = 304.0k.

Through a reseller. The reseller keeps a 30% commission, so Keelson receives 32.0k x 70% = 22.4k. Keelson still serves the customer, so subtract 8.0k: 14.4k a customer. The reseller does the selling, so there is no cost to win. Each customer is worth 4.6k less than a direct one; the reseller has to bring enough extra customers to cover that. Its reach is a multiple of Keelson's direct customers, between 1.2 and 2.6, most likely 1.8.

One exclusive licensee. Keelson receives the larger of a 300.0k minimum guarantee and 20% of the licensee's revenue. The licensee has between 20 and 90 customers, most likely 45, at 32.0k each, and pays no cost to serve to Keelson because the licensee serves them. The share overtakes the guarantee only when 20% x customers x 32.0k is above 300.0k: 300.0k / (20% x 32.0k) = 300.0k / 6.4k = 46.9 customers. The most likely licensee has 45, so the guarantee is doing real work.

Self-studyWhat each formula leaves out2 min▶

These are one steady-state year, with no build cost, no platform run cost and no years of ramp-up. Those belong to the five-year desk in Analyst session 6, which adds them back. Tonight the question is narrower: once the product exists, which route turns it into the most money a year, and how sure can you be?

Two simplifications to say out loud. The reseller sells at Keelson's list price; real marketplaces sometimes discount, which moves the 22.4k. And the exclusive route assumes the licensee charges its own customers the same 32.0k; a licensee who bundles the index into a bigger product may charge more or less, and your share moves with it. The contract terms in Part 3 are how you pin those down.

Real world

Three offers on Sefa's desk. A data marketplace offered to list the index for a 30% commission and do all the selling. A fleet-telematics firm asked for the index exclusively, so that none of its competitors could buy it, and offered a 300.0k minimum guarantee with a 20% revenue share on top. Keelson's own option was a small direct sales effort. Ines Carvalho, the CFO, asked for one thing before any meeting: the worst ordinary year of each route, on one page. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Part 2 · covers the simulation

Three ranges on one axis

5 min live

The three formulas each have an input nobody knows: how many customers Keelson signs, how far the reseller reaches, how big the licensee grows. So the widget draws each route 5,000 times from those ranges and prints the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile of the year's result. The same seed, 20261033, fixes the draws, so every laptop in the room prints the same table.

LiveReading the table3 min▶

Sell direct: P10 +240.2k, P50 +304.8k, P90 +366.9k, mean +304.0k. The narrowest range: Keelson controls every step and the only unknown is how many customers it signs.

Through a reseller: P10 +270.4k, P50 +429.0k, P90 +629.8k, mean +440.3k. The highest middle and the widest spread. Even its P10 sits above the direct P10.

One exclusive licensee: P10 +300.0k, P50 +323.8k, P90 +462.2k, mean +354.7k, at the guarantee in 41.0% of draws. The highest floor, and the floor is exactly the guarantee. In four draws out of ten the licensee is small enough that the guarantee is what Keelson gets.

So the answer depends on the question. "Which route earns most in a typical year?" The reseller. "Which route can we least afford to see go badly?" The exclusive has the best bad year. A CFO and a head of partnerships can both be right about the same table.

Self-studyHow the draws are tied together2 min▶

Each draw is one random number that sets every uncertain input of a route at once. In the reseller route that means a draw with few direct customers also has a short reach: the two move together, which is a strong assumption and makes the reseller's spread wider than if they were independent. Each input is drawn from a triangular range (low, most likely, high), the simplest honest shape when you have three numbers and no data.

The method itself, why a range beats a single number, what P10 and P90 mean to a funder, and why a fixed seed matters, is taught in Business Cases & ROI, Analyst session 5 ↗. Use it here; do not rebuild it.

Real world

The page Ines asked for. Sefa brought the three ranges to the CFO. Ines Carvalho read the bottom row first: the exclusive could not do worse than +300.0k, because the guarantee is written into the contract. Then she read the reseller's middle, +429.0k, and asked what the exclusive gave up to buy that certainty. That question is Part 3. Constructed Keelson Mutual example.

Part 3 · covers floors, caps and the terms that carry them

A guarantee is a floor, exclusivity is a cap

5 min live

The minimum guarantee lifts the bottom of the range; that is what Keelson buys. Exclusivity is what Keelson pays for it: a cap on everything it could have sold to anyone else. In a thin market the floor wins. In a strong one the cap bites. On the desk in Analyst session 6, the sponsor's case sold direct ends five years at +1362.4k; the same case with the exclusive licence ends at +953.4k. The guarantee did not change between those two runs; the market did.

LiveSix clauses that carry the numbers3 min▶

Minimum guarantee and true-up. The guarantee is the floor; the true-up is how the share above it gets paid, and when. Without a true-up date, a share that beats the guarantee can arrive late or not at all.

Field of use and territory. What the licensee may use the index for, and where. A narrow field leaves Keelson free to sell the same index to repair networks or parts makers outside it.

Exclusivity scope. Exclusive against whom: one segment (fleet operators), one region, for a fixed time. "Exclusive" with no scope is a cap on the whole market forever. This is the clause that decides whether +953.4k or +1362.4k is closer to your future.

Derived data at the end. What happens to models, tables and products the licensee built from the index when the licence ends. If they keep them, the licensee no longer needs to renew, and the cap reaches past the term.

Audit rights. A revenue share is only as large as the revenue you can verify. The right to inspect the licensee's customer counts and invoices is what makes the 20% real.

Term and renewal. How long, how it renews, and who may walk away. The range in Part 2 is one year; the end of a term is when the whole of it can stop.

Self-studyNo law sets the price of a voluntary deal2 min▶

The EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854), which applies from 12 September 2025 with some parts staggered into 2026 and 2027, has rules on terms and price in Articles 8 and 9: fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms, and reasonable compensation that "may include a margin". Those articles apply only where a data holder is obliged by law to make data available. Keelson licensing its index to a partner it chose is a voluntary deal, so the price, the guarantee and the share are whatever the two sides negotiate. Do not cite Articles 8 and 9 as a pricing rule for a voluntary licence.

What still applies is everything Analyst session 2 settled: the index may only contain what Keelson may lawfully pass on, and the licence chain carries that limit into the partner's contract. The field of use clause is where the inbound limits become outbound ones.

Real world

Two licensees, and both terms ending. Reddit's annual report for fiscal 2025 (10-K) says its data licensing revenue came from two partners. Its quarterly report filed on 30 October 2026 (10-Q) says those agreements are "approaching the end of their respective current terms". Two buyers is a concentration risk even when both pay well: the term and renewal clause, not the price, decides what next year looks like. An exclusive licence is the extreme case, with one buyer by design.

Homework

Before Analyst session 6

30 min
  1. Write one route as one line. For a data product you know, write the direct per-customer margin as price minus cost to serve minus cost to win. If you cannot fill a term, that is the number to go and find.
  2. Find the break point. For one exclusive offer, real or imagined, divide the guarantee by the share times the price. That is the number of licensee customers at which the share overtakes the floor. Is the licensee's likely size above or below it?
  3. Read one partner contract. Find a licence or reseller agreement your organisation has signed, for data or anything else, and copy its exclusivity scope and its term and renewal clause word for word.
  4. Count your buyers. List the buyers of one data product or service. What share of its revenue comes from the largest two? Write one sentence on what happens if both reach the end of their terms in the same year.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Analyst session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Keelson's exclusive offer carries a 300.0k minimum guarantee. What does the guarantee do to the range of a year's result?

The guarantee is a floor written into the contract. It lifts the bottom of the range and leaves the top to the revenue share.

2 · With the sponsor's inputs on the desk, selling direct ends five years at +1362.4k and the exclusive licence at +953.4k. What does that gap show?

The guarantee wins in a thin market and caps a strong one. In the sponsor's case the market is strong, so the cap costs more than the floor is worth.

3 · Reddit's FY2025 10-K says its data licensing revenue came from two partners, and its 10-Q filed 30 October 2026 says those agreements are approaching the end of their current terms. Why is that a risk?

Concentration means the term and renewal clause carries most of the risk. An exclusive licence takes that to one buyer by design.
